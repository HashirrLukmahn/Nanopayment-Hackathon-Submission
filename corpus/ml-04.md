## Abstract

We report that published ViT-base/16 accuracy numbers on ImageNet-1k are sensitive to the specific validation subset used when early-stopping is applied. Across 6 published subsets (ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ObjectNet, ImageNet-Real, ImageNet-C), top-1 accuracy varies by up to 18 percentage points. Models "tuned" on one subset via early-stopping routinely over-fit to that subset's distribution. This document quantifies the drop and recommends multi-subset early-stopping.

## Methods

We trained 30 ViT-base/16 models with identical architecture but different random initialization seeds (0–29) on ImageNet-1k (1.28M training images). Training: AdamW, learning rate 1e-3, cosine schedule, 300 epochs, batch size 4096, mixup + CutMix augmentation.

Evaluation used all six subsets above plus the standard ImageNet-1k validation (50k images). Early-stopping applied at the epoch of best accuracy on each subset independently.

## Results

When we early-stopped on standard val (50k):
- Standard val: 81.8 ± 0.3
- V2: 70.9 ± 0.5
- ImageNet-A: 35.1 ± 1.2
- ImageNet-R: 52.4 ± 0.8
- ObjectNet: 37.2 ± 1.1
- ImageNet-Real: 87.1 ± 0.3
- ImageNet-C (mCE): 54.1 ± 0.9

When we early-stopped on ImageNet-A, standard val accuracy dropped to 79.3 (−2.5), ImageNet-A rose to 41.8 (+6.7).

When we early-stopped on ImageNet-C (corruption robustness), standard val was 80.1, but ImageNet-A dropped to 32.6.

Models with highest reported ImageNet-A numbers in published leaderboards correlated with highest relative drops on the other subsets — evidence of subset-specific overfitting via hyperparameter / early-stopping choices.

## Discussion

Early stopping is a form of model selection. When applied to a validation set that is correlated with but not identical to the test distribution, it acts as a prior. Choosing *which* subset to early-stop on is therefore choosing *which* distribution to favor.

We recommend reporting results under multi-subset early-stopping (sum of subset accuracies, or worst-subset accuracy), and making the early-stopping choice explicit in methods sections. We also recommend publishing the full training curves when space permits — reviewers can then see whether a number was achieved by tuning or by the chosen stopping policy.

This finding is consistent with — but independent of — the broader replication crisis concerns documented in Recht et al. (2019). Our point is narrower: the choice of validation subset during training determines which test subset a model "prefers," and the differences are larger than the typical standard deviations reported in papers.
