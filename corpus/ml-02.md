## Abstract

We attempted to train StyleGAN2-ADA on a dataset of 18,000 dermoscopic images for synthetic data augmentation in melanoma classification. Despite applying the standard stabilization toolkit (R1 regularization, non-saturating loss, path length regularization, mini-batch standard deviation, differentiable augmentation), we observed persistent mode collapse: at step 800k, the generator produced approximately 40 visually distinct images regardless of the latent vector. This document reports the full hyperparameter sweep and the partial rescue we achieved with an auxiliary classifier loss.

## Methods

Architecture: StyleGAN2-ADA (official PyTorch implementation, commit f15c83e). Dataset: aggregated from ISIC-2019 and ISIC-2020 challenges, filtered to 512×512 dermoscopic images, manually cleaned for 18,247 final images across 8 diagnostic classes.

Training: 8× A100 GPUs, batch size 256 (32 per GPU), R1 gamma swept across {1, 5, 10, 20, 50}, path length regularization standard settings, ADA augmentation probability adapted as specified by Karras et al.

All runs were for 25M images (equivalent to ~1400 epochs). Evaluation: FID, KID, coverage via nearest-neighbor assignment in feature space, and visual inspection every 400k images.

## Results

Default R1 gamma = 10: FID dropped from 245 to 31 over the first 5M images, then plateaued. Coverage metric dropped from 0.95 (noisy random) to 0.17 by 10M images — confirming mode collapse. Generated images clustered into ~40 distinct modes regardless of latent z.

R1 gamma sweep: Gamma = 1 → FID 28, coverage 0.14. Gamma = 50 → FID 42, coverage 0.31 (some diversity rescue but poor quality). Gamma = 20 → FID 34, coverage 0.22.

Increasing dataset from 18k to 36k images (via horizontal flips + 90° rotations in augmentation) did not help — collapse occurred at similar step counts.

Adding an auxiliary classifier loss (pre-trained melanoma classifier, λ = 0.1 weighting) rescued coverage to 0.58 but FID degraded to 58 — the generator started chasing class boundaries at the cost of realism.

## Discussion

We hypothesize the dataset's tight mode distribution (many near-duplicate benign nevi) makes the discriminator's task easy, and the generator finds "shortcut" modes that satisfy the discriminator locally. The auxiliary classifier partially breaks the shortcut by requiring class balance, but the loss landscape becomes less smooth.

Practical lessons: (1) dermoscopic data is not diverse enough for pure-unconditional StyleGAN training; (2) class-conditional + auxiliary loss is a better starting point for medical imaging; (3) FID alone is misleading — always report coverage-style metrics when diversity matters.

For teams considering StyleGAN for medical synthesis, we recommend starting with conditional architectures (e.g. class-conditional StyleGAN3) or diffusion models (latent diffusion, Stable Diffusion fine-tuning) which in our follow-up experiments showed 3× higher coverage at matched FID.
