## Abstract

We report the negative in vivo outcome of a GPR-88 agonist chemistry program constrained by severe aqueous solubility deficits. Across 38 analogues, only four exhibited kinetic solubility above 1 μM in PBS pH 7.4; none exceeded 10 μM. Oral bioavailability in C57BL/6 mice was <5% for all tested compounds at 10 mg/kg; increasing dose to 100 mg/kg did not scale F% proportionally. The program was terminated at the lead-optimization stage. This document is intended to help teams working on adjacent Gαi-coupled receptors avoid the same failure mode.

## Methods

Kinetic solubility in PBS pH 7.4, FaSSIF, and FeSSIF was measured by shake-flask with 4-hour equilibration and LC-UV quantitation. Thermodynamic solubility used a 24-hour equilibration and XRPD confirmation of crystal form. LogD was measured by shake-flask at pH 7.4 and 2.0.

Mouse PK was conducted in male C57BL/6 mice (n=3 per cohort) with IV (2 mg/kg, DMSO/PEG400/saline 10/40/50) and PO (10 or 100 mg/kg, 0.5% methylcellulose suspension). Plasma samples were analyzed by LC-MS/MS with a lower limit of quantitation of 1 ng/mL.

## Results

LogD7.4 values clustered around 4.1–5.8, consistent with poor aqueous behavior. Kinetic solubility was <0.5 μM for 34/38 compounds. Melting points exceeded 220°C for the active analogues — indicating a crystal lattice energy penalty.

In PK: mean Cmax was 12–40 ng/mL at 10 mg/kg PO (versus expected 400–600 ng/mL based on IV exposure and projected F%). The 10×-higher PO dose produced only a 2.4× Cmax increase on average, consistent with dissolution-limited absorption in the gut.

FaSSIF solubility was 3–8× higher than PBS for the active series, but this did not translate to in vivo exposure gains meaningful for Gαi signaling. Lipid-based formulations (SNEDDS) produced a further 2–3× exposure bump but still below projected efficacious levels.

## Discussion

The dominant issue is crystal energy, not lipophilicity per se. Several analogues (e.g. GPR-088-017, GPR-088-024) had LogD < 3.5 but still showed <1 μM solubility due to tight π-stacking observed by single-crystal X-ray. Attempts to disrupt stacking via methylation of the central aryl core reduced melting point and boosted solubility ~4× but abolished GPR-88 binding (Ki shifted from 18 nM to >3 μM).

We conclude that the binding pharmacophore and the π-stacking that kills solubility are mutually entangled at the level of ring electronics. Teams should consider bioisosteres early — pyridazine → pyrimidine → pyrazine swaps may break the stacking without destroying binding — and should not rely on LogD as a proxy for solubility in rigid flat scaffolds.
