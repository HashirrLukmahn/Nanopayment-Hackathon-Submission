## Abstract

We report the failure of intranasal lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-encapsulated mRNA constructs encoding anti-amyloid single-chain antibody fragments (scFv) to achieve therapeutically relevant CNS concentrations in three transgenic murine Alzheimer's disease models (5×FAD, APP/PS1, 3×Tg-AD). Despite strong mucosal uptake and robust systemic protein expression, brain parenchymal scFv concentrations remained below the 1 nM threshold estimated for amyloid-β plaque clearance across all tested formulations, routes, and dose schedules over a 12-week study. These negative results close off a promising, non-invasive administration strategy and redirect future work toward direct CNS delivery or transcytosis-enhancing modifications.

## Background

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) represents the primary delivery obstacle for CNS-targeted biologics. Intranasal administration had been proposed as a means to exploit the olfactory and trigeminal nerve pathways to bypass the BBB, with several groups reporting low-level CNS delivery of small molecules and peptides via this route. We sought to extend this approach to mRNA-LNP constructs following the successful demonstration of systemic mRNA therapeutics in clinical settings.

## Methods

### Animal Models
Three transgenic lines were used (n=12 per group, equal sex distribution):
- 5×FAD (overexpresses mutant human APP and PSEN1)
- APP/PS1 (Swedish APP mutation + PSEN1 L166P)
- 3×Tg-AD (APP Swedish + PSEN1 M146V + tau P301L)

All experiments conducted at 6 months of age, prior to advanced plaque burden, to maximise the theoretical therapeutic window.

### LNP Formulations Tested
Four ionisable lipid formulations were evaluated:
1. **F1** — MC3-based (DLin-MC3-DMA), PEGylated, benchmark formulation
2. **F2** — ALC-0315 analogue with olfactory mucosa-optimised surface charge (ζ = −5 mV)
3. **F3** — Novel adamantane-conjugated lipid designed for tight junction interaction
4. **F4** — F2 + 1% RVG peptide surface decoration (rabies virus glycoprotein, known neurotropism)

Intranasal dosing: 5 µL per naris, bilateral, 10 µg mRNA total, twice weekly. Comparator arm: intracerebroventricular (ICV) injection at 2 µg mRNA.

### Endpoints
- Brain parenchymal scFv concentration (ELISA, weeks 4, 8, 12)
- Plasma scFv concentration (ELISA, same timepoints)
- Olfactory bulb fluorescence (luciferase mRNA reporter, IVIS imaging)
- Amyloid-β plaque burden (thioflavin-S staining, weeks 8, 12)
- Mucosal toxicity (nasal lavage cytology, H&E histology)

## Results

### CNS Delivery Failure
Brain parenchymal scFv was undetectable (<0.05 nM) in all intranasal groups (F1–F4) at all timepoints in all three models. Positive ICV control arm achieved 14.2 ± 3.1 nM at week 4, confirming the assay sensitivity and construct functionality.

Olfactory bulb luciferase signal was detectable in 9/12 animals per group at week 4, confirming mucosal uptake and local mRNA translation. Signal did not propagate beyond the olfactory bulb in any animal; cortical and hippocampal signal was indistinguishable from background.

### Robust Systemic Expression
Plasma scFv concentrations were robust across all formulations:
- F1: 18.4 ± 4.2 nM
- F2: 22.1 ± 5.8 nM
- F3: 16.9 ± 3.9 nM
- F4: 24.3 ± 6.1 nM

This confirms LNPs achieved vascular access (likely via nasal vasculature and lymphatics) but plasma-origin scFv did not cross the BBB in detectable quantities.

### No Plaque Reduction
Thioflavin-S quantification revealed no significant difference in plaque burden between intranasal groups and vehicle controls at week 8 or 12 (p > 0.05, one-way ANOVA with Tukey correction). ICV arm showed 31% plaque reduction at week 12 (p < 0.001 vs. vehicle).

### Safety
No mucosal toxicity or histological abnormalities were observed at any dose. Nasal lavage cytology was normal across all groups and timepoints. No weight loss or behavioural changes were noted.

## Discussion

The olfactory bulb represents a local translation compartment, not a gateway to broader CNS parenchyma for LNP cargo. Our RVG-decorated formulation (F4) — which exploits the same neurotropism mechanism as rabies virus — also failed to propagate beyond the olfactory epithelium and bulb, suggesting that the transcytosis pathway exploitable by whole virus is not accessible to the LNP particle size range tested (80–120 nm hydrodynamic diameter).

The systemic expression data indicate that the nose-to-brain pathway does not contribute meaningfully to plasma scFv; instead, nasal vasculature provides rapid systemic absorption. This suggests future work should explicitly distinguish "nasal → CNS via olfactory nerve" from "nasal → systemic → CNS" with appropriate BBB-impermeant tracers.

### Implications for the Field

These results argue strongly against pursuing intranasal mRNA-LNP as a CNS delivery strategy without one of the following modifications:

1. **Transcytosis ligands at smaller particle sizes** — particles <30 nm with LRP1 or transferrin receptor ligands may traverse brain capillary endothelium from the systemic side
2. **Focused ultrasound BBB opening** — transient sonoporation combined with systemic mRNA-LNP may enable parenchymal delivery
3. **Direct CNS administration** — ICV or intrathecal delivery bypasses the BBB entirely; the potency demonstrated here (31% plaque clearance via ICV) justifies further tolerability work

## Conclusions

Intranasal mRNA-LNP constructs do not deliver therapeutically relevant concentrations of encoded protein to brain parenchyma in murine AD models, despite strong local and systemic expression. This strategy should be considered closed for this indication without substantive reformulation targeting active BBB transcytosis or physical opening strategies.
