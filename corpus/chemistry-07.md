## Abstract

We report systematic radioligand-binding assay failures that delayed a PAR-2 (protease-activated receptor 2) antagonist discovery program by 9 months. Tritiated tracer [3H]-PAR-2-27 (in-house synthesis) failed to show saturable binding in either CHO-K1 or HEK293 stable PAR-2 lines despite repeated membrane preparations. Eventual resolution required a shift to fluorescent ligand binding (BODIPY-PAR-2-33) and identified a non-obvious lipid-dependence in receptor folding. This is a methods-focused negative result intended to save time for teams working on similarly lipid-dependent GPCRs.

## Methods

Stable CHO-K1 and HEK293 lines expressing human PAR-2 were generated by lentiviral transduction; expression confirmed by flow cytometry (anti-PAR-2 antibody SAM11, 85-92% positive). Membranes were prepared using sucrose-gradient ultracentrifugation.

Radioligand binding used [3H]-PAR-2-27 at 1–20 nM, non-specific binding determined with 10 μM cold competitor. Scintillation counting (Beckman LS 6500). All assays run in duplicate, three biological replicates.

Fluorescent ligand binding used BODIPY-PAR-2-33 at 0.5–50 nM, measured by microplate fluorescence and high-throughput flow cytometry.

## Results

Radioligand binding in CHO-K1 membranes: Bmax < 0.1 pmol/mg protein, Kd non-convergent in fitting. No measurable antagonist competition observed.

Radioligand binding in HEK293 membranes: Bmax < 0.05 pmol/mg protein. Same non-convergence.

Whole-cell radioligand binding (skip membrane prep): Bmax = 0.3 pmol/million cells in CHO-K1, Kd = 3.2 nM — binding was detectable on intact cells but lost during membrane preparation.

Fluorescent ligand binding in whole cells: Kd = 2.8 nM in CHO-K1, 4.1 nM in HEK293, Bmax normalized per receptor expression. Competitor IC50 values reproducible across 3 technicians.

## Discussion

The failure of radioligand binding in membrane preparations but not whole cells suggested a lipid-environment issue. Further analysis (cholesterol depletion by methyl-β-cyclodextrin, 10 mM, 30 min) reduced whole-cell binding Bmax by 78% — indicating strong cholesterol-dependence of PAR-2 binding.

The membrane preparation protocol had likely removed essential cholesterol-rich lipid rafts. Shifting to fluorescent ligand on whole cells bypassed the issue. Retrospectively, we could have prepared membranes using a "cold" (4°C) sonication protocol without detergent, which subsequent experiments showed preserves binding.

For teams working on GPCRs with known raft localization (PAR-2, CCR5, β2-AR in some preparations), we recommend: (1) whole-cell binding as a primary assay when feasible, (2) if membrane prep is required, include cholesterol repletion (5% cholesterol/CHAPS or MβCD-cholesterol saturation), (3) early validation by cholesterol depletion as a positive control for raft-dependence.
